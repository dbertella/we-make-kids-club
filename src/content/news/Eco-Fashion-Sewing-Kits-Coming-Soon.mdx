Who said sewing kits for children need to be made from plastic or childish?

I know you can find very cheap basic sewing kits, but does it really contain what they need or is it too much/not enough? How long do you think it will last?

I wanted to make my own sewing kits for children using exactly what they've been using in my classes and what I know works for them.

So here are the kits I have been working on in the past weeks:

**My Little Sewing Kit**![](</little sewing kit.jpg>)

In My Little Sewing Kit, I chose:

* **Needles** (x2): a big needle, perfect for younger children or less experienced ones, and just easier to use with yarn; and a smaller needle, for thicker fabrics.

<!---->

* **Pins** (x3): to get you started and keep in place those two pieces of fabrics you're trying to sew together.

<!---->

* **Needle threader**: I chose one in aluminium here, as the ones with the wire we often use tends to break easily,  I also like that it has a big hook for yarn and big needles and a smaller hook for thread and smaller needles

<!---->

* **Threads** (3 types): most mass-produced kits include only one type of thread, the very thin one (and don't get me started on the quality). It's fine to sew a button, but for children who get started, they much more prefer working with thicker tread. So I included 3 types in my kit: some yarn to get you started, practice your stitches or comfortably sew a fidget toy for example; some embroidery thread, great for... you guessed it, embroidery, they can practice with their name or tiny shapes; and some thin thread for more advanced sewing projects.

<!---->

* **Safety pin**: when upcycling clothes, you'll find having a safety pin is incredibly useful. It allows you to thread a ribbon or large piece of string through your fabric. All the examples below have had a safety pin involved!

![](</upcycling usinf a safety pin.jpg>)

And last but not least, the **handmade pouch**. I wanted to make these kits PRECIOUS. Yes, that's right. Not your typical "made in China" kit. I still use fabric remnants and donated fabrics (to reduce waste), but I also take the time to select fabric and lining carefully for the best combination and I make each one by hand so they are all unique.

These kits are no toys, children can already get better habits by mending and upcycling their clothes if they have the right tools, that's why I wanted their sewing kit to be "precious" and well-made.

**Stitch a Softie Kit**![](</stitch a softie kit.jpg>)

Children have made tons of softies in my classes and even my 10-11 year olds pupils were asking for more. Sometimes I've let them make their own patterns but I found it was more helpful to give them a base to work with. So I created a few easy patterns for them to use and it really triggered inspiration and creativity among them.

In Stitch a Softie Kit, I chose:

* a **beautiful handmade pouch** that they can reuse
* **rescued fabrics** for the softie
* **yarn**
* **needle**
* **instructions booklet** (including step-by-step guide and colourful illustrations)
* **my favourite softie pattern + 2 extras** (to inspire them to make more)
* a **sticker** "I love Eco-Fashion"
