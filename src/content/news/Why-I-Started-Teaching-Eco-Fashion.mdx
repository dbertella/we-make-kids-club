I'll start with an apology, I'm not very regular or consistent with my blog posts as I just end up writing when I remember to do so and find some inspiration.

Yesterday, a student in my eco-fashion club asked me this direct question: "why did you start an eco-fashion club and why did you want to transform old clothes into new ones?" 

Unfortunately (or fortunately) for that student, I didn't get the chance to give them a full answer as the sessions are always quite heated, with children asking for support/advice (I always try to keep a mental note of the next 3-5 children who have been calling my name while I'm helping one of them already!). So I ended up babbling something like: "Well, you know, I studied fashion design and I always liked to make things..."

The reality is that, it feels obvious to me now, even if it's still new (I started in 2025), there's no short answer.

## A mix of fashion background

I started transforming my clothes when I was in high school and I realised everyone was wearing whatever they wanted and I fell finally free to express myself. I was studying literature, languages and art. And, as the pressure grew to decide what I wanted to do as a career, I felt naturally attracted to fashion design.

My time at the Lycee de la Mode in Cholet (France) was amazing and I learned so much. I was thriving transforming my clothes using the new skills I was learning and the professional equipment I had access to. However, making clothes from scratch was less exciting (the precision, the calculations, the rules...) and a (fashion) world dominated by money and relationships quickly became less and less attractive.

While I didn't work in fashion after that, I kept making things during my free time. I kept making clothes for a while. Then I made jewellery. I painted a lot. Then I started writing and illustrating. And finally... the mid-life crisis kicked in!

## And mid-life crisis

After over 10 years working in digital marketing, I decided I needed to do something creative. But what? I tried so many different creative projects already! I started to be aware of a growing fashion sustainable movement and I decided I wanted to be part of it, so I started sourcing second-hand denim jackets to paint on. Incredibly satisfying, but too time-consuming and I was not solving a problem, just making something to sell. One day, I was reflecting on some children that I know, who, very young, started asking about clothes they want to buy (especially girls). And I thought, this is a problem I could work on. As a parent, you just want to make your child happy and buy the (pretty) things they want. But this consumerism will impact the rest of their lives. What if I could help it? With my knowledge and my passion for upcycling, what if I could teach the new generations to turn their old clothes into new ones? 

## Why sustainability matters

It's not just about teaching children how to sew. It's about giving them the skills and confidence to express themselves and reuse what they already have. I went to school assemblies to tell all the children how many clothes are produced by fast-fashion brands and how many are discarded every day in the world, how much water it takes to make a single cotton t-shirt, and where these millions of unwanted clothes end up. The facts are scary, the numbers are shocking and the climate change is getting worse, with its consequences terrifying. What if I could make a difference, even if just locally?

The best part of teaching children eco-fashion? Witnessing the excitement of  Seeing the pride in the children's eyes when they finish a project. Or maybe hearing a parent who tells me how I inspired their child to do more eco-fashion at home.
